# T2 bundled source set — Thalliline-doped sodium-flow batteries (Brennecke Institute)

All four sources below are fictional, authored for Sabot. Nothing here describes a real
material, institution, or publication.

---

## Source A — Brennecke Institute press release (dated 3 February 2030)

The Brennecke Institute today announced first characterization of a thalliline-doped
sodium-flow cell, a redox-flow chemistry in which a thalliline dopant stabilizes the
negative electrolyte against crossover. In accelerated cycling the prototype "reached
12,000 charge-discharge cycles before falling below 80% capacity," which the Institute
described as a step-change for grid-scale storage. Measured gravimetric energy density
was 148 Wh/kg. The Institute named its lead chemist, Dr. Ivo Karamanlis, as principal
investigator and said an independent teardown audit had been commissioned.

## Source B — Conference abstract, 14th Grid-Storage Materials Symposium (April 2030)

Karamanlis et al., "Crossover suppression in thalliline-doped Na-flow cells." The
abstract reports a projected stack-level cost of **$61/kWh** at 500 MWh manufacturing
scale, and a gravimetric energy density of **148 Wh/kg**, consistent with the Brennecke
prototype. The abstract notes a hard operating-temperature floor: below **4 °C** the
thalliline dopant precipitates out of the negative electrolyte and cycling must be
suspended. Cycle-life claims are deferred to the pending independent audit.

## Source C — Independent teardown audit, Halvorsen-Pike Laboratories (dated 9 September 2030)

Halvorsen-Pike Laboratories conducted the commissioned independent audit of the
Brennecke thalliline-doped sodium-flow prototype. Re-running the accelerated cycling
protocol under controlled conditions, the audit measured **8,400 cycles** to the 80%
capacity knee — not the 12,000 figure quoted in the February press release. **The
12,000-cycle figure is retracted; it reflected an un-normalized depth-of-discharge and
should not be cited as the cell's cycle life.** Energy density (148 Wh/kg) and the 4 °C
operating floor were both confirmed. The audit stands by 8,400 cycles as the corrected,
governing cycle-life figure.

## Source D — Trade-press explainer, *Storage Ledger Weekly* (October 2030)

A plain-language explainer for industry readers. Sodium-flow batteries decouple power
(stack size) from energy (tank size), which is why the Brennecke team pursued the
thalliline dopant: it cuts electrolyte crossover, the main aging path in sodium-flow
designs. The piece notes the cycle-life figure "was revised downward after an
independent audit" and that the chemistry's Achilles' heel is cold weather, echoing the
4 °C floor. It credits the Brennecke Institute as the originator.
