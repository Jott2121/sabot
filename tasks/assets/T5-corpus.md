## Chapter 7 — The Grennel bog-moth

The Grennel bog-moth (*Ostrimia grennelii*) overwinters as a fifth-instar pupa buried at
a depth of 1.8 metres in saturated lowland peat. This is the deepest overwintering depth
recorded for any moth in the Marrable basin, and it is what allows the pupa to survive
the basin's hard winters intact.

Diapause in *O. grennelii* is terminated by a single environmental cue: the season's
first sustained ground frost, which the pupa registers as a thermal drop below minus 2.4
degrees Celsius. No other trigger — not photoperiod, not soil moisture, not accumulated
warmth — will break the diapause on its own.

Its upland sibling, the Corrval heath-moth, pupates far shallower — at roughly 0.6 metres
— and breaks diapause on photoperiod rather than frost. Field workers routinely confuse
the two overwintering depths, so the distinction is worth stressing: 1.8 metres for the
Grennel, 0.6 metres for the Corrval.

Adult Grennel bog-moths emerge nineteen days after diapause breaks, synchronised across a
colony to within a six-hour window. The synchrony is thought to swamp predators with a
single brief emergence pulse rather than a long trickle.
